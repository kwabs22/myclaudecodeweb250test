# 100 Guitar Music Theory Questions

A comprehensive set of questions to test and reinforce your understanding of music theory concepts as they apply to guitar playing and programming guitar parts in a DAW.

---

## Basic Guitar Knowledge (Questions 1-10)

### 1. What are the notes of the guitar strings in standard tuning, from lowest to highest?
**Answer:** E-A-D-G-B-E (6th string to 1st string)

### 2. If you play the 5th fret of the 6th string (low E), what note are you playing?
**Answer:** A (same pitch as the open 5th string)

### 3. How many semitones does moving up one fret represent?
**Answer:** 1 semitone (half step)

### 4. What note is located at the 12th fret of any string?
**Answer:** The same note as the open string, one octave higher

### 5. In standard tuning, which two adjacent strings are NOT tuned a perfect 4th apart?
**Answer:** The G (3rd) and B (2nd) strings - they are a major 3rd apart (4 semitones instead of 5)

### 6. What is the MIDI note number range for a standard 6-string guitar in standard tuning?
**Answer:** E2 (MIDI note 40) to approximately E6 (MIDI note 88), depending on how many frets the guitar has

### 7. If you're in Drop D tuning, what note is the 6th string tuned to?
**Answer:** D (one whole step down from E)

### 8. What interval is created when you play the same fret on two adjacent strings (except G and B strings)?
**Answer:** Perfect 4th

### 9. In an open G tuning (D-G-D-G-B-D), what chord do you get when strumming all open strings?
**Answer:** G major chord

### 10. What are the five shapes in the CAGED system?
**Answer:** C, A, G, E, and D chord shapes that can be moved up and down the neck

## Guitar Scales and Patterns (Questions 11-20)

### 11. How many positions/patterns are there for the minor pentatonic scale across the fretboard?
**Answer:** 5 positions (one for each note of the pentatonic scale)

### 12. In the A minor pentatonic scale (A-C-D-E-G), what is the "blue note" that creates the blues scale?
**Answer:** Eb (D#) - the flatted 5th

### 13. If you're playing position 1 of A minor pentatonic starting at the 5th fret, which frets will you primarily use?
**Answer:** Frets 5-8 (with most notes on frets 5 and 7)

### 14. What scale degree pattern defines the major scale?
**Answer:** W-W-H-W-W-W-H (Whole-Whole-Half-Whole-Whole-Whole-Half)

### 15. In the "3 notes per string" scale pattern, how many positions are needed to cover the major scale across the entire neck?
**Answer:** 7 positions (one starting on each scale degree)

### 16. What mode would you be playing if you use the C major scale pattern but resolve to D?
**Answer:** D Dorian mode

### 17. If you play only the white keys on piano (C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C) on guitar starting from the 8th fret of the 6th string, what key are you in?
**Answer:** C major (or A minor)

### 18. What is the formula for the harmonic minor scale?
**Answer:** W-H-W-W-H-W+H-H (or 2-1-2-2-1-3-1 in semitones)

### 19. Which scale has the pattern: whole step - whole step - whole step - whole step - whole step - whole step?
**Answer:** Whole tone scale

### 20. In E minor pentatonic (E-G-A-B-D), which notes from the full E natural minor scale are omitted?
**Answer:** F# and C (the 2nd and 6th scale degrees)

## Guitar Chords and Voicings (Questions 21-30)

### 21. What three notes make up an E major power chord (E5) when played with root on the 6th string?
**Answer:** E (root), B (perfect 5th), E (octave)

### 22. In a barre chord using the E major shape moved to the 3rd fret, what chord are you playing?
**Answer:** G major

### 23. What notes comprise an open C major chord in standard voicing (X-3-2-0-1-0)?
**Answer:** C-E-G-C-E (from 5th string to 1st string, with 6th string muted)

### 24. Why do power chords work well with distortion?
**Answer:** They contain no 3rd, avoiding dissonant overtones; only root and 5th create clean, powerful sound

### 25. If you play strings 4-3-2 at frets 7-7-7, what chord are you playing?
**Answer:** D major triad (D-A-D-F#)

### 26. What is a "cowboy chord"?
**Answer:** An open position chord (typically C, A, G, E, D, Am, Em, Dm) played in first position

### 27. In the CAGED system, which shape would you use to play a C major chord with the root on the 5th string, 3rd fret?
**Answer:** A shape (moved to the 3rd fret)

### 28. What is the difference between Cmaj7 and C7 chords?
**Answer:** Cmaj7 has a major 7th (B), while C7 has a minor 7th (Bb)

### 29. If you play a sus4 chord and resolve it, which note moves and where does it go?
**Answer:** The 4th moves down by one semitone to the 3rd (e.g., in Csus4, F moves down to E)

### 30. What notes would you play for a shell voicing of Gmaj7 (just 3rd and 7th)?
**Answer:** B (major 3rd) and F# (major 7th)

## Guitar Techniques in Piano Roll (Questions 31-40)

### 31. When programming a downstroke strum in piano roll, in what order should the notes trigger?
**Answer:** Lowest to highest (bass strings first, treble strings last), with 3-5ms spacing

### 32. What velocity range is appropriate for the second note in a hammer-on?
**Answer:** 60-80 (lower than the picked note which would be 100-127)

### 33. How do you represent a whole-step bend in piano roll?
**Answer:** Start on one note, use pitch bend automation to raise it 2 semitones, or play the target note with pitch bend from below

### 34. What note length setting creates an authentic palm-muted sound in piano roll?
**Answer:** Very short/staccato (typically 1/16 note or shorter with gaps between notes)

### 35. When programming a pull-off from B to A, how should the velocities compare?
**Answer:** First note (B) should have higher velocity (picked), second note (A) should have lower velocity (pulled-off)

### 36. How far apart should notes be spaced when programming a realistic guitar strum?
**Answer:** 3-5 milliseconds between each string

### 37. For two-hand tapping, what is the typical velocity relationship between picked, tapped, and pulled-off notes?
**Answer:** Picked (100-127), tapped (90-110), pulled-off (70-90)

### 38. When creating a slide guitar part with portamento, should you use stepped pitches or continuous glide?
**Answer:** Continuous glide/portamento for smooth sliding between pitches

### 39. How do you create realistic artificial harmonics in piano roll?
**Answer:** Play notes 1-2 octaves higher than the fretted position, often at 12th fret (octave), 7th fret (octave+5th), or 5th fret (2 octaves)

### 40. What's the best way to humanize programmed guitar strums?
**Answer:** Slight timing variations, varied note spacing (3-5ms), subtle velocity differences, occasional ghost notes

## Applied Guitar Music Theory (Questions 41-50)

### 41. Why are the keys of E, A, D, G, and C considered "guitar-friendly"?
**Answer:** They utilize open strings, allowing for easier fingering, fuller resonance, and characteristic guitar tone

### 42. What is the musical interval between the 5th fret of the 6th string and the 7th fret of the 5th string?
**Answer:** Perfect 5th (A to E)

### 43. If you're playing in E minor and want to add a modal interchange chord from E major, what chord could you borrow?
**Answer:** A major (IV from E major) or C# minor (vi from E major), among others

### 44. In a I-IV-V progression in the key of A, what chords would you play?
**Answer:** A major - D major - E major

### 45. What is the relationship between the A minor pentatonic scale and the C major pentatonic scale?
**Answer:** They contain the same notes (they are relative scales - Am is the relative minor of C major)

### 46. If you're playing a 12-bar blues in E, what are the three main chords?
**Answer:** E7 (I), A7 (IV), and B7 (V)

### 47. What interval creates the distinctive sound of a major chord versus a minor chord?
**Answer:** The 3rd - major 3rd (4 semitones) sounds happy, minor 3rd (3 semitones) sounds sad

### 48. In Drop D tuning, what advantage does the low D provide for playing power chords?
**Answer:** You can play power chords on the bottom 3 strings with just one finger barred across one fret

### 49. If you're playing in the Dorian mode starting on A (A Dorian), what major scale shares the same notes?
**Answer:** G major (A Dorian is the 2nd mode of G major)

### 50. When programming a guitar solo in A minor, which notes from the A blues scale create the most tension and typically resolve downward?
**Answer:** The blue note (Eb/D#) creates tension and typically resolves down to D; the G can also create tension resolving down to F or E

## Advanced Guitar Techniques (Questions 51-60)

### 51. What is a pinch harmonic and how would you represent it in piano roll?
**Answer:** A pinch harmonic is created by lightly touching the string with the thumb while picking, producing a high-pitched squeal. In piano roll, use very high notes (2-3 octaves above fretted position) with short duration and pitch bend vibrato.

### 52. Explain the difference between natural vibrato and mechanical vibrato on guitar.
**Answer:** Natural vibrato = slight pitch variations created by finger movement (±1/4 to 1/2 semitone). Mechanical vibrato = tremolo/whammy bar creates wider pitch variations. Program with pitch bend automation: subtle (±50 cents) for natural, wider for whammy bar.

### 53. What is sweep picking and how does it affect note timing in piano roll?
**Answer:** Sweep picking is a technique where you use one continuous pick motion across multiple strings. Notes should be very close together in timing (1-3ms apart) with consistent velocity (90-110) to create the fluid, fast arpeggiated sound.

### 54. How do you program a realistic guitar pick scrape in piano roll?
**Answer:** Create a descending chromatic or atonal run with very short note durations, low velocity (30-50), and rapid timing. Often starts high and descends to low strings, with increasing velocity at the end.

### 55. What is economy picking and how does it differ from alternate picking?
**Answer:** Economy picking = pick moves in same direction when crossing strings (more efficient). Alternate picking = strict down-up-down-up pattern. In piano roll, economy picking might have slight timing variations on string crosses, while alternate is more metronomic.

### 56. How would you program a chicken picking (hybrid picking) pattern in piano roll?
**Answer:** Combination of picked notes (higher velocity 100-120) and plucked notes (medium velocity 80-100). Country/funk style uses syncopated rhythms with sharp staccato notes and occasional ghost notes (velocity 40-60).

### 57. What is a pre-bend and how do you create it in piano roll?
**Answer:** Bend the string before picking so the note starts at target pitch. In piano roll: use pitch bend automation set to +2 semitones (or desired interval) before note trigger, then optionally release during or after the note.

### 58. Explain the "rake" technique and its velocity characteristics.
**Answer:** Rake = quick muted notes leading to a target note, creating percussive attack. Program as 2-4 very short muted notes (velocity 20-40, very short duration) immediately before the main note (velocity 100-127).

### 59. What is tapping harmonics and what MIDI note range represents it?
**Answer:** Tapping at harmonic nodes (12th, 7th, 5th fret positions) produces bell-like tones. Use MIDI notes 2-3 octaves above normal fretted position, often with modulation and delay effects. Very short attack with some sustain.

### 60. How do you differentiate between a dive bomb and a scoop in pitch bend programming?
**Answer:** Dive bomb = pitch bend downward from played note (start at 0, bend down to -12 or more semitones). Scoop = start below target pitch and bend up into it (start at -2 to -5 semitones, bend to 0).

## Modes and Advanced Scales (Questions 61-70)

### 61. What makes the Phrygian mode sound "Spanish" or "exotic"?
**Answer:** The flatted 2nd scale degree (b2) creates a distinctive half-step between root and 2nd, characteristic of flamenco and Spanish music. In E Phrygian: E-F-G-A-B-C-D.

### 62. How would you use the Lydian mode over a IV chord to create a "dreamy" sound?
**Answer:** The #4 (or #11) in Lydian creates floating, ethereal quality. Over F major in key of C, use F Lydian (F-G-A-B-C-D-E) emphasizing the B natural for the characteristic sound.

### 63. What is the Mixolydian mode and over which chord type is it commonly used?
**Answer:** Major scale with b7. Formula: W-W-H-W-W-H-W. Used over dominant 7th chords (e.g., G Mixolydian over G7). Creates bluesy, rock sound. The b7 provides dominant function.

### 64. What scale would you use to solo over a half-diminished chord (m7b5)?
**Answer:** Locrian mode (7th mode of major scale). For Bm7b5, use B Locrian (B-C-D-E-F-G-A) which has b2, b3, b5, and b7 - matching the chord tones.

### 65. Explain the difference between melodic minor ascending and the Dorian mode.
**Answer:** Melodic minor has raised 6th and 7th vs natural minor. Dorian has raised 6th only. A melodic minor ascending: A-B-C-D-E-F#-G#. A Dorian: A-B-C-D-E-F#-G.

### 66. What is the Phrygian dominant scale and in what genres is it commonly used?
**Answer:** 5th mode of harmonic minor. Has b2, major 3rd, and b6. Formula: H-W+H-H-W-H-W. Creates Middle Eastern, metal, and flamenco sounds. E Phrygian dominant: E-F-G#-A-B-C-D.

### 67. How do you form a diminished scale and what is its symmetrical property?
**Answer:** Alternating half and whole steps. Two types: half-whole (H-W-H-W...) and whole-half (W-H-W-H...). Symmetrical = repeats every minor 3rd (3 frets), so only 3 unique diminished scales exist.

### 68. What is the altered scale (Super Locrian) and when would you use it?
**Answer:** 7th mode of melodic minor. Contains all altered tones: b9, #9, #11, b13. Used over altered dominant chords (7alt, 7#5, 7b9). Creates maximum tension before resolution.

### 69. What makes the harmonic major scale unique?
**Answer:** Major scale with b6. Creates exotic sound similar to harmonic minor but from major scale. C harmonic major: C-D-E-F-G-Ab-B. The W+H interval (G-Ab-B) creates distinctive sound.

### 70. Which scale shares all the same notes as C major but starts on A?
**Answer:** A natural minor (Aeolian mode). This is the relative minor relationship - all white keys. A-B-C-D-E-F-G.

## Chord Theory and Extensions (Questions 71-80)

### 71. What notes comprise a Cmaj9 chord and how would you voice it on guitar?
**Answer:** C-E-G-B-D (root, 3rd, 5th, 7th, 9th). Guitar voicing often omits 5th: C-E-B-D or E-B-D-G (from low to high), using easier fingerings.

### 72. What is a slash chord and how is C/E different from C major?
**Answer:** Slash chord = chord with specific bass note. C/E means C major chord with E in bass (first inversion). Written as E-G-C from low to high instead of C-E-G.

### 73. How do you construct an add9 chord and how does it differ from a 9th chord?
**Answer:** Add9 = triad + 9th, no 7th. Cadd9: C-E-G-D. A 9th chord (Cmaj9 or C9) includes the 7th. Add9 has brighter, more open sound without 7th.

### 74. What is a 6/9 chord and in what style is it commonly used?
**Answer:** Major triad + 6th + 9th, no 7th. C6/9: C-E-G-A-D. Jazz, bossa nova, and pop use it for sophisticated, non-dominant endings. Sweet, stable sound.

### 75. Explain the function of a secondary dominant chord.
**Answer:** Dominant chord that resolves to a chord other than tonic. In C major, D7 (V7/V) resolves to G (V). Creates temporary key change feeling, adds harmonic interest.

### 76. What is a tritone substitution and how does it work?
**Answer:** Replace V7 with bII7 (dominant chord a tritone away). In C major, replace G7 with Db7, both resolve to Cmaj. They share the same 3rd and 7th (enharmonically).

### 77. How do you voice a drop 2 chord voicing on guitar?
**Answer:** Take close position chord, drop 2nd note from top down an octave. Cmaj7 close: C-E-G-B becomes E-C-G-B (drop 2). Creates wider, fuller voicing common in jazz.

### 78. What is a quartal voicing and how does it differ from tertian harmony?
**Answer:** Built in 4ths instead of 3rds. C quartal: C-F-Bb-Eb (stacked 4ths). Sounds open, modern, ambiguous. Jazz and fusion use extensively. Tertian = traditional 3rd-based triads/7ths.

### 79. What makes a dominant 13th chord different from other 13th chords?
**Answer:** Dominant 13th (G13) has minor 7th, making it dominant function. Contains: G-B-D-F-A-C-E (R-3-5-b7-9-11-13). Major 13th (Gmaj13) has major 7th. Typically omit 11th.

### 80. How do you create a sus2 to major resolution on guitar?
**Answer:** Replace 3rd with 2nd, then resolve to major 3rd. Csus2 (C-D-G) → C major (C-E-G). The D resolves up to E. Creates tension and release, common in rock/pop.

## Rhythm and Timing (Questions 81-90)

### 81. What is a gallop rhythm and how do you program it in guitar parts?
**Answer:** Metal rhythm: two quick notes followed by longer note. Pattern: 8th-16th-16th (or 16th-16th-8th). Program with power chords, palm muting, tight timing. Example: E-E-E (long-short-short).

### 82. How do you create a reggae guitar skank rhythm in piano roll?
**Answer:** Staccato chords on off-beats (the "and" of each beat). Mute strings heavily (very short notes, 1/16 or shorter), medium velocity (60-80). Typically on beats 2 and 4 in 4/4.

### 83. What is a polymetric pattern and give an example for guitar?
**Answer:** Two different meters played simultaneously. Example: 3/4 pattern over 4/4 time signature. Creates interesting rhythmic tension. Tool and King Crimson use extensively.

### 84. Explain the difference between swing at 66% and straight 16th notes.
**Answer:** Straight 16ths = evenly spaced. Swing (66% or 2:1 ratio) = first of each pair is twice as long. At 66%, first note gets 2/3 of beat, second gets 1/3. Creates triplet feel.

### 85. What is a hemiola and how would you create it on guitar?
**Answer:** Rhythmic pattern suggesting 3/2 time over 6/8 (or vice versa). Example: 6/8 time with accents every 3 beats creates 3/4 feel. Common in Spanish and Latin music.

### 86. How do you program a realistic funk guitar rhythm with ghost notes?
**Answer:** Syncopated 16th note pattern with accents on off-beats. Ghost notes (velocity 20-40) between accented notes (velocity 90-120). Tight, percussive, palm-muted sound. Staccato.

### 87. What is a displaced accent and how does it create groove?
**Answer:** Accent on unexpected beat. Instead of accenting 1 and 3, accent "&" of 2 or other off-beats. Creates syncopation and forward motion. Common in funk and jazz.

### 88. How do you create a latin clave-based guitar rhythm?
**Answer:** Follow 3-2 or 2-3 clave pattern. 3-2 Son clave timing: beats 1, 2.5, 4, 1.5, 3. Program guitar chords or bass notes on clave hits with slight anticipation for authentic feel.

### 89. What is the shuffle feel and what's the mathematical ratio?
**Answer:** Triplet-based feel where 8th notes are played as first and third note of triplet. Ratio is 2:1 (first note twice as long). Different from straight 16ths. Blues, jazz standard.

### 90. How do you program a realistic palm-muted breakdown rhythm for metal?
**Answer:** Tight 16th or 32nd notes on low strings with very short duration (staccato), medium-low velocity (70-90), following kick drum pattern. Often uses syncopation and rests for impact.

## Genre-Specific and Performance Questions (Questions 91-100)

### 91. What chord progression defines the 12-bar blues and what are the chords in E?
**Answer:** I-I-I-I-IV-IV-I-I-V-IV-I-V (or I). In E: E7-E7-E7-E7-A7-A7-E7-E7-B7-A7-E7-B7. Each numeral represents one bar.

### 92. What makes a jazz ii-V-I progression sound more sophisticated than I-IV-V?
**Answer:** Uses diatonic chord function with 7th chords, creates stronger voice leading. In C: Dm7-G7-Cmaj7. The tritone in G7 (B-F) resolves to 3rd and 7th of Cmaj7.

### 93. How would you voice a Hendrix-style dominant 7#9 chord?
**Answer:** E7#9 (Hendrix chord): E-G#-D-G (low to high). Contains both major 3rd (G#) and minor 3rd (enharmonic to #9). Creates bluesy, funky dissonance. Play with distortion.

### 94. What scale and approach notes work best over a flamenco progression in E Phrygian?
**Answer:** E Phrygian (E-F-G-A-B-C-D) or E Phrygian dominant (E-F-G#-A-B-C-D). Emphasize b2 (F) and major 3rd (G#). Use rapid chromatic runs and ornaments.

### 95. In country guitar, what are the characteristics of a chicken-picked lick?
**Answer:** Hybrid picking (pick + fingers), fast 16th note patterns, string skipping, open string drones. Bright, percussive sound. Often in Mixolydian or major pentatonic with chromatic approaches.

### 96. How do you program a realistic metal gallop with triplet variation?
**Answer:** Combination of 16th note gallops and 16th note triplets. Palm-muted low string power chords, tight timing. Velocities 95-120. Example: 16th-16th-8th followed by triplet on next beat.

### 97. What defines a bossa nova guitar comp pattern?
**Answer:** Syncopated pattern in 2/4 or 4/4 with emphasis on off-beats. Soft fingerstyle or light pick. Jazz voicings (maj7, m7, 6/9). Pattern: bass note on 1, chord on 2&, bass on 3, chord on 4.

### 98. In blues-rock, how do you create the "Eric Clapton" tone programmatically?
**Answer:** Combination of technique and effects: string bends with vibrato, minor pentatonic + blues scale, medium velocity (75-95), slight pitch bend vibrato at phrase ends, use of the b5 (blue note).

### 99. What makes a surf rock guitar rhythm distinctive?
**Answer:** Fast tremolo picking on single notes or dyads, reverb-heavy sound, staccato notes, often in minor keys. Rapid 16th or 32nd notes with consistent velocity (90-100). Think Dick Dale.

### 100. How would you program a progressive rock odd-meter riff like Tool or Dream Theater?
**Answer:** Use time signatures like 7/8, 11/8, or 13/16. Palm-muted power chords with polymetric patterns. Accent pattern creates groove (e.g., 7/8 as 3+2+2). Tight timing, medium-high velocity (90-110).
