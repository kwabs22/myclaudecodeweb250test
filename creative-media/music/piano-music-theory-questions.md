# 100 Piano Music Theory Questions

A comprehensive set of questions to test and reinforce your understanding of music theory concepts as they apply to piano playing and programming piano parts in a DAW.

---

## Basic Piano Knowledge (Questions 1-10)

### 1. How many keys does a standard piano have?
**Answer:** 88 keys (52 white keys and 36 black keys)

### 2. What is the name of the white key immediately to the left of any group of two black keys?
**Answer:** C (the "landmark" note for finding your position on the keyboard)

### 3. What is Middle C in scientific pitch notation, and what is its MIDI note number?
**Answer:** C4 in scientific pitch notation, MIDI note 60

### 4. What pattern of black keys helps you locate the note F on the piano?
**Answer:** F is immediately to the left of the group of three black keys

### 5. How many octaves does a standard 88-key piano span?
**Answer:** Just over 7 octaves (A0 to C8)

### 6. What is the interval between any white key and the black key immediately to its right?
**Answer:** One semitone (half step) - the black key is the sharp of the white key

### 7. Which two pairs of white keys have no black key between them?
**Answer:** E-F and B-C (these are natural semitones)

### 8. What is the typical playing range for most pop and rock piano parts?
**Answer:** C3 to C6 (three octaves centered around middle C)

### 9. If you play C and E together, how many semitones apart are they?
**Answer:** 4 semitones (a major 3rd)

### 10. What are the white keys on a piano named in order?
**Answer:** C-D-E-F-G-A-B, then the pattern repeats

## Piano Scales and Theory (Questions 11-20)

### 11. What is the fingering pattern for a C major scale in the right hand?
**Answer:** 1-2-3-1-2-3-4-5 (thumb crosses under after finger 3)

### 12. How many sharps or flats are in the key of C major?
**Answer:** Zero - C major has no sharps or flats (all white keys)

### 13. What is the relative minor of C major?
**Answer:** A minor (shares the same key signature, starts on the 6th degree)

### 14. In the key of G major, which note is sharped?
**Answer:** F# (the 7th degree of the scale)

### 15. What scale uses only the black keys on the piano?
**Answer:** F# major pentatonic (or Gb major pentatonic) - also the enharmonic Db major pentatonic

### 16. What is the formula for the harmonic minor scale in whole and half steps?
**Answer:** W-H-W-W-H-W+H-H (whole-half-whole-whole-half-whole&half-half)

### 17. If you start on D and play only white keys, what mode are you playing?
**Answer:** D Dorian (the 2nd mode of C major)

### 18. What interval is created by playing C and G together?
**Answer:** Perfect 5th (7 semitones)

### 19. How many notes are in a pentatonic scale?
**Answer:** 5 notes (penta = five)

### 20. In a chromatic scale starting on C, what is the 7th note?
**Answer:** F# or Gb (C-C#-D-D#-E-F-F#...)

## Piano Chords and Voicings (Questions 21-30)

### 21. What three notes make up a C major triad in root position?
**Answer:** C-E-G (root, major 3rd, perfect 5th)

### 22. What is the difference between a C major chord and a C minor chord?
**Answer:** The 3rd - C major has E (major 3rd), C minor has Eb (minor 3rd)

### 23. What four notes comprise a Cmaj7 chord?
**Answer:** C-E-G-B (root, major 3rd, perfect 5th, major 7th)

### 24. What is a "shell voicing" for a C7 chord?
**Answer:** Playing just the essential tones: C (root), E (3rd), and Bb (7th), often omitting the 5th

### 25. If you play C-F-G, what type of chord is this?
**Answer:** Csus4 (suspended 4th chord - replaces the 3rd with the 4th)

### 26. In a first inversion C major chord, which note is in the bass?
**Answer:** E (the 3rd becomes the lowest note: E-G-C)

### 27. What is a "10th" interval, and why is it important in piano voicing?
**Answer:** An octave plus a 3rd (17 semitones); important because it creates fuller sound than a 3rd when voiced in bass register

### 28. What notes would you play for a rootless Cmaj9 voicing (common in jazz)?
**Answer:** E-G-B-D (3rd, 5th, 7th, 9th - omitting the root C)

### 29. What is the difference between a rolled chord and a blocked chord?
**Answer:** Rolled = notes played in quick succession (arpeggiated); Blocked = all notes played simultaneously

### 30. In stride piano, what does the left hand typically play on beats 1 and 3?
**Answer:** Bass notes (root or root and 5th of the chord)

## Piano Technique in Piano Roll (Questions 31-40)

### 31. When programming an Alberti bass pattern in C major, what is the note order?
**Answer:** C-G-E-G (root-5th-3rd-5th, repeated)

### 32. What MIDI CC number controls the sustain pedal?
**Answer:** CC64 (values 0-63 = off, 64-127 = on)

### 33. What velocity range represents piano (soft) playing?
**Answer:** Approximately 40-60 (pp to p dynamic range)

### 34. How should you program a realistic glissando in piano roll?
**Answer:** Rapid succession of notes (white or black keys) with slight timing offset (2-5ms apart) and increasing velocity

### 35. For a Chopin-style waltz accompaniment in 3/4 time, what is the left hand pattern?
**Answer:** Bass note on beat 1, chord on beats 2 and 3

### 36. When programming a piano tremolo, what note duration should you use?
**Answer:** Very short notes (1/32 or 1/64) repeated rapidly, with slight velocity variation

### 37. How do you create a realistic rolled chord ascending in piano roll?
**Answer:** Offset each note by 5-15ms, starting with lowest note, ending with highest

### 38. What velocity value typically represents fortissimo (very loud) playing?
**Answer:** 100-127 (ff to fff dynamic range)

### 39. For authentic two-hand independence, what should you avoid in piano roll programming?
**Answer:** Impossible stretches (more than an octave per hand), overlapping hands in impossible ways, or patterns that require more than 10 fingers

### 40. How should sustain pedal be used when changing chords to avoid muddiness?
**Answer:** Release pedal briefly during chord changes (syncopated pedaling), then re-engage after new chord starts

## Applied Piano Music Theory (Questions 41-50)

### 41. In a ii-V-I progression in C major, what are the three chords?
**Answer:** Dm7 (ii) - G7 (V) - Cmaj7 (I)

### 42. What is the purpose of using a Vsus4 to V resolution?
**Answer:** Creates tension and release; the 4th resolves down to the 3rd, strengthening the pull to the I chord

### 43. In stride piano, what interval is typically played on beat 2 of the left hand?
**Answer:** A 10th (octave + 3rd) or sometimes just a chord

### 44. What is the I-V-vi-IV progression in the key of F major?
**Answer:** F - C - Dm - Bb

### 45. Why should you avoid playing small intervals (2nds, 3rds) in the bass register (below C3)?
**Answer:** They create muddy, unclear sound due to low frequency overtones clashing

### 46. What is "comping" in jazz piano?
**Answer:** Rhythmic, syncopated chord accompaniment that supports a soloist, typically using shell voicings on off-beats

### 47. In gospel piano, where do runs typically occur in relation to the beat?
**Answer:** Leading into the downbeat (usually on beat 4, resolving to beat 1)

### 48. What is the characteristic of a Lydian mode that makes it sound "dreamy"?
**Answer:** The raised 4th (#4 or #11), which creates an augmented 4th from the root

### 49. When using parallel motion (planing), why does it work despite breaking traditional voice leading rules?
**Answer:** Creates impressionistic, colorful sound; uniform motion prevents individual voices from standing out, creating a cohesive harmonic wash

### 50. In a montuno pattern (Latin piano), which hand typically plays the repetitive syncopated figure?
**Answer:** Right hand (while left hand plays bass tumbao pattern)

## Bonus: Advanced Concepts (5 Challenge Questions)

### 51. What is the difference between sostenuto pedal (CC66) and sustain pedal (CC64)?
**Answer:** Sostenuto sustains only notes already being held when pedal is pressed; sustain pedal sustains all notes played while pedal is down

### 52. In a diminished 7th chord, what is the interval between each consecutive note?
**Answer:** Minor 3rd (3 semitones) - creates a symmetrical chord structure

### 53. What makes a "Neapolitan sixth" chord unique in classical harmony?
**Answer:** It's a major chord built on the lowered 2nd scale degree, typically used in first inversion

### 54. How do you voice a quartal chord, and what genre commonly uses it?
**Answer:** Stack notes in 4ths (e.g., C-F-Bb-Eb); common in jazz and modern classical music

### 55. What is the relationship between C Lydian and G major scale?
**Answer:** They share the same notes - C Lydian is the 4th mode of G major

## Advanced Piano Voicings (Questions 56-65)

### 56. What is a drop 3 voicing and how does it differ from drop 2?
**Answer:** Drop 3 = take close position chord, drop 3rd note from top down an octave. Cmaj7 close: C-E-G-B becomes G-C-E-B (drop 3). Wider spread than drop 2, often used in solo piano.

### 57. How do you voice a So What chord (Dm11) like Bill Evans?
**Answer:** Stacked 4ths/5ths: D-G-C-F-A or E-A-D-G-C. Quartal voicing with omitted 3rd. Creates open, modern jazz sound. Use both hands - left plays lower notes, right plays upper.

### 58. What is a cluster chord and in what context would you use it?
**Answer:** Adjacent notes played together (e.g., C-D-E or C-Db-D). Creates dissonance/tension. Used in modern classical, jazz, and cinematic music for dramatic effect or texture.

### 59. How do you voice a rootless A section voicing for a ii-V-I in C?
**Answer:** Dm7: C-E-F-A (3-5-6-8). G7: B-E-F-A (3-6-7-9). Cmaj7: B-E-G-A (7-3-5-6). Left hand plays roots, right hand plays guide tones and extensions without roots.

### 60. What is the difference between close and open position voicing?
**Answer:** Close position = all notes within an octave (C-E-G-B in one octave). Open position = notes spread beyond an octave (C3-G3-E4-B4). Open position sounds fuller, less muddy.

### 61. How would you voice a Kenny Barron-style Maj7#11 chord?
**Answer:** Use both hands: Left hand - root and 7th (C-B). Right hand - 3rd, #11, 9th (E-F#-D). Creates lush, sophisticated sound. The #11 adds Lydian color.

### 62. What is a spread voicing and how do you create one?
**Answer:** Voicing with intervals larger than an octave between some voices. Example: C3-G3-E4-B4-D5 for Cmaj9. Creates spacious, orchestral sound. Common in solo piano and ballads.

### 63. Explain the upper structure triad concept in jazz piano.
**Answer:** Play a triad in right hand over left hand root/shell. For G13: G (root) + D major triad (D-F#-A-C# = 5-7-9-13). Creates colorful extensions efficiently.

### 64. How do you voice a diminished 7th chord to create maximum tension?
**Answer:** Use all four notes close together (e.g., C-Eb-Gb-A) for compact tension. Or spread across hands for dramatic effect. Symmetrical = can resolve to multiple keys.

### 65. What is the McCoy Tyner quartal voicing style?
**Answer:** Stacked perfect 4ths in left hand, melodic line in right. Example: left hand plays D-G-C-F, right hand improvises. Creates powerful, modal jazz sound. Omits 3rds.

## Scales, Modes, and Improvisation (Questions 66-75)

### 66. What is the bebop scale and how does it create smooth lines over changes?
**Answer:** Major or dominant scale with added chromatic passing tone. C bebop major: C-D-E-F-G-G#-A-B. The 8-note scale aligns chord tones on downbeats when playing 8th notes.

### 67. How do you practice two-octave scales with proper fingering for both hands?
**Answer:** Right hand usually 1-2-3-1-2-3-4-5 (thumb crosses under 3). Left hand often 5-4-3-2-1-3-2-1 (crosses over). Practice hands separately first, then together.

### 68. What modal interchange options exist when playing in C major?
**Answer:** Borrow from C minor, C Dorian, C Mixolydian, etc. Common borrowed chords: Fm, Ab, Bb, Eb from C minor. Creates emotional depth while staying in "C."

### 69. In what order should you practice the modes for maximum understanding?
**Answer:** Start with most common: Ionian (major), Aeolian (minor), then Dorian, Mixolydian, Phrygian, Lydian, finally Locrian. Focus on characteristic note of each mode.

### 70. How do you use the melodic minor scale in jazz improvisation?
**Answer:** Use same scale ascending and descending (unlike classical). Creates access to altered dominant sounds. A melodic minor over E7alt provides all alterations (b9, #9, #11, b13).

### 71. What is the symmetrical augmented scale and over what chord is it used?
**Answer:** Repeating pattern of half-whole-half steps. C augmented: C-Db-E-F-Ab-A. Used over augmented triads. Symmetrical = only 4 unique augmented scales total.

### 72. How do you approach chord-scale relationships in modern jazz?
**Answer:** Match scale to chord quality: Major 7 = Ionian or Lydian, m7 = Dorian or Aeolian, 7 = Mixolydian or altered, m7b5 = Locrian. Consider harmonic context.

### 73. What is the blues scale and how does it differ from minor pentatonic?
**Answer:** Minor pentatonic + b5 (blue note). C blues: C-Eb-F-Gb-G-Bb. The Gb creates characteristic blues tension and can be approached/resolved chromatically.

### 74. How do you create a convincing gospel piano run connecting I to IV?
**Answer:** Use chromatic approach notes and scale tones. C to F: C-D-Eb-E-F or C-Db-D-E-F. Start slow, accelerate into the IV chord. End on chord tone. Increase velocity.

### 75. What is the Lydian Chromatic Concept and how does it apply to piano?
**Answer:** George Russell's theory: Lydian is the "unity" of a chord. Any chord can be approached through its Lydian mode. Creates outside/inside harmonic possibilities.

## Rhythm, Groove, and Style-Specific Patterns (Questions 76-85)

### 76. How do you program an authentic New Orleans second-line piano rhythm?
**Answer:** Syncopated 16th notes with bass line walking on 1-3, chords on off-beats. Left hand: root-5th pattern with syncopation. Right hand: punctuated chords and runs. Swing feel.

### 77. What defines a classical Baroque-style left hand pattern (Alberti bass)?
**Answer:** Broken chord pattern: lowest-highest-middle-highest. In C: C-G-E-G repeated. Provides harmonic rhythm and motion. Mozart and Haydn used extensively. Keeps steady pulse.

### 78. How do you create an authentic boogie-woogie bass line?
**Answer:** 8-to-the-bar pattern using walking bass with triplets or straight 8ths. Common pattern: root-3rd-5th-6th or root-3rd-5th-b7. Stays in lower register (C2-C3). Driving rhythm.

### 79. What is the clave son pattern and how does it apply to piano montuno?
**Answer:** 3-2 clave: X..X...X.X...X.. (or 2-3 reverse). Montuno follows this rhythmic pattern. Right hand plays syncopated chord pattern, left hand plays tumbao (bass pattern) aligned with clave.

### 80. How do you program realistic rubato in a romantic piano piece?
**Answer:** Gradual tempo changes before important notes/chords. Slight ritardando at phrase endings, accelerando building tension. Use tempo automation or manual note placement off grid.

### 81. What characterizes a Fats Waller stride piano left hand pattern?
**Answer:** Strong bass notes on 1 & 3 (often using 10ths), mid-range chords on 2 & 4. Left hand "strides" across keyboard. Bouncy, rhythmic feel. Right hand improvises/states melody.

### 82. How do you create an authentic ragtime syncopation?
**Answer:** Right hand plays syncopated melody (notes on off-beats, especially before beat 1). Left hand maintains steady boom-chick (bass-chord) pattern. Creates "ragged time" feel.

### 83. What is the característ ica left hand pattern in Brazilian bossa nova piano?
**Answer:** Syncopated bass line emphasizing beats 1, 2&, 3, 4. Often plays root on 1, 5th on 2&, root on 3, chromatic approach on 4&. Creates samba-influenced groove.

### 84. How do you program a convincing church/gospel two-hand rhythmic pattern?
**Answer:** Both hands play chords rhythmically together with syncopation. Heavy use of sus4 resolutions, major-minor voice movement. Emphasis on 2 & 4. Often uses triplet feels and fills.

### 85. What defines a classical waltz accompaniment pattern beyond just 3/4 time?
**Answer:** Bass note on beat 1 (root or root+5th), chord on beats 2-3. Often oom-pah-pah feel. Right hand may add melody or countermelody. Chopin adds harmonic sophistication.

## Advanced Techniques and Effects (Questions 86-95)

### 86. How do you program a realistic piano glissando with dynamics?
**Answer:** Rapid succession of notes (white or black keys only), starting slow and accelerating. Velocity starts medium (60-70), increases to high (100-120). Spacing: start 10ms apart, end 2ms apart.

### 87. What is the difference between a trill and a tremolo in classical piano?
**Answer:** Trill = rapid alternation between two adjacent notes (e.g., C-D-C-D). Tremolo = rapid repetition of single note OR alternation between two notes of any interval. Both create sustained effect.

### 88. How do you create a convincing grace note (acciaccatura) in MIDI?
**Answer:** Very short note (1/64 or shorter) just before main note (1-10ms before), low velocity (40-60), usually one semitone above or below target. Quickly resolves to main note.

### 89. What is a Viennese trill and how does it differ from a regular trill?
**Answer:** Starts on upper auxiliary note, then main note alternation. Regular trill starts on main note. Viennese: D-C-D-C-D-C vs regular C-D-C-D-C-D. Classical performance practice.

### 90. How do you program sympathetic resonance using MIDI CC?
**Answer:** Use sostenuto pedal (CC66) to hold bass notes while playing melody staccato above. Or extend certain note lengths while sustain pedal is modulated. Creates harp-like shimmer effect.

### 91. What is the una corda pedal effect and how is it different from just reducing velocity?
**Answer:** Una corda (soft pedal, CC67) shifts hammers to strike fewer strings, changing timbre to softer, mellower tone. Velocity reduction only changes volume, not timbral quality.

### 92. How do you create a piano cluster glissando like in contemporary classical music?
**Answer:** Multiple adjacent notes (forearm cluster) sliding chromatically up or down. Program as chromatic runs with 3-5 simultaneous notes moving in parallel. High velocity, chaotic feel.

### 93. What is inside-the-piano extended technique and how could you represent it?
**Answer:** Plucking strings, hitting strings with mallets, etc. In MIDI: use unusual velocity ranges, very short/long notes, pitch bends, and combine with non-piano sounds. Experimental timbres.

### 94. How do you program a realistic piano string dampening effect?
**Answer:** Very short note durations (staccato) with release time at 0. Velocity 40-70 for muted effect. Or automation of sustain pedal to create choked notes. Percussive, muted timbre.

### 95. What is half-pedaling and how do you represent it in MIDI?
**Answer:** Sustain pedal partially depressed (CC64 value 40-60 instead of 0-127). Creates subtle sustain without full resonance. Allows harmonic clarity while maintaining some connection between notes.

## Final Advanced Concepts (Questions 96-100)

### 96. What is a Neapolitan sixth chord and how would you voice it on piano?
**Answer:** bII major chord in first inversion. In C major: Db major chord (Db-F-Ab) with F in bass. Voicing: F2-Db3-F3-Ab3. Resolves powerfully to V or I. Classical dramatic device.

### 97. How do you voice and resolve an Italian augmented sixth chord?
**Answer:** In C major: Ab-C-F# (bVI, I, #IV). Resolves outward to G (V). Left hand: Ab2, right hand: C-F#. Both voices move outward by half-step to G. Creates powerful dominant preparation.

### 98. What is a French augmented sixth chord and how does it function?
**Answer:** bVI-I-II-#IV. In C: Ab-C-D-F#. Creates dominant preparation. Resolves to V (G). More dissonant than Italian aug 6th due to added D (major 2nd).

### 99. How do you use parallel dominant 13th chords (planing) for modern jazz sound?
**Answer:** Move entire 13th chord shape chromatically. C13 shape (C-E-G-Bb-D-A) moved up to Db13, D13, etc. Breaks voice leading rules but creates lush, cinematic harmony.

### 100. What is the difference between functional and non-functional harmony in piano performance?
**Answer:** Functional = chords have directional pull (V→I, ii→V). Non-functional = chords chosen for color/texture (modal, planing). Functional uses voice leading; non-functional uses parallel motion or stepwise bass.
