## South Africa 🇿🇦

### National Crisis as Meme Fuel

#### **Load Shedding (Eskom Crisis)**
- **What it is:** Scheduled power cuts implemented by Eskom (state-owned power utility)
- **Started:** 2007, ongoing crisis
- **Stages:** Ranges from Stage 1 to Stage 6+ (higher = more cuts)
- **Cultural Impact:** Defined South African life for nearly two decades

**Meme Culture:**
- **"Eishkom"** - Portmanteau of "Eish" + "Eskom"
- Load shedding memes help South Africans "get through the darkest of days" (literally)
- **Common Themes:**
  - Candles and torches as lifestyle
  - Inverter and generator culture
  - "When Stage 6 hits"
  - Planning life around load shedding schedules
  - Productivity jokes (can't work without power)
  - Food spoilage tragedy
- **Academic Recognition:** 2024 study "Laughing When the Lights Go Out: Humour and the Electricity Crisis in South Africa"
- **National Coping Mechanism:** Humor about detrimental effects on businesses and homes

**Load Shedding Stage Memes:**
- Each stage represented with increasingly apocalyptic imagery
- Stage 1-2: Minor inconvenience
- Stage 3-4: Serious concern
- Stage 5-6: Societal collapse vibes
- Stage 6+: "Mad Max" South Africa

### South African Slang

#### **"Eish"**
- **Pronunciation:** "Aysh"
- **Meaning:** Expression of surprise, annoyance, resignation, or sympathy
- **Usage:** All-purpose exclamation
- **Cultural Status:** Quintessentially South African
- **Origin:** Became iconic from 2005 Klipdrift brandy ad
- **Examples:**
  - "Eish, load shedding again"
  - "Eish, that's rough"
  - "Eish, what can you do?"
- **Flexibility:** Can express almost any emotion depending on tone

#### **"Yebo"**
- **Meaning:** "Yes" (from Zulu/Xhosa)
- **Cultural Icon:** "Yebo Gogo" Vodacom campaign (early 2000s)
- **Impact:** One of most beloved South African ad series
- **Usage:** Affirmative response, agreement
- **Variations:** "Yebo yes" - emphatic agreement

#### **"Howzit"**
- **Full Form:** "How is it?"
- **Usage:** THE South African greeting
- **Meaning:** "Hello" more than "How are you?"
- **Cultural Significance:** Universal SA greeting
- **Response:** Often "Lekker" or "Sharp"
- **International Confusion:** Foreigners think it's asking about their wellbeing

#### **"Lekker"**
- **Origin:** Afrikaans for "tasty"
- **Meaning:** Nice, good, great, fun, enjoyable
- **Usage:** Everything positive is "lekker"
- **Examples:**
  - "Lekker weather"
  - "That's lekker"
  - "Having a lekker time"
- **Cultural Truth:** "Everything in South Africa is lekker"

#### **"Boet" / "Bru"**
- **Origin:** Afrikaans "broer" (brother)
- **Meaning:** Male friend, dude, mate
- **Usage:** Term of affection between males
- **Variations:**
  - Boet
  - Bru
  - Boetie (diminutive, more affectionate)
- **Context:** Very casual, friendly

#### **"Robot"**
- **Meaning:** Traffic light
- **Confusion Level:** Extreme for foreigners
- **Usage:** "Turn left at the robot"
- **Cultural Quirk:** One of SA's most distinctive terms
- **NOT:** Humanoid machines, vacuums, or AI

#### **"Just Now" / "Now Now"**
- **"Just now":** Later (could be 5 minutes or 5 hours)
- **"Now now":** Very soon (but not immediately)
- **"Now":** Immediately (actually means now)
- **Foreign Confusion:** Causes endless misunderstandings
- **Cultural Reality:** Flexible time concepts

#### **"Sharp"**
- **Meanings:**
  - Cool, great
  - Okay, understood
  - Goodbye
- **Variations:**
  - "Sharp sharp" - Very good, definitely
  - "Sharp left/right" - Quick turn
- **Versatility:** Used in many contexts

#### **"Ag"**
- **Pronunciation:** Like "ach" in German
- **Meaning:** Oh, ah, expressing various emotions
- **Usage:**
  - "Ag no, man" - Oh no
  - "Ag shame" - Aww, how sweet/sad
- **Untranslatable:** Pure Afrikaans influence

#### **"Shame"**
- **SA Meaning:** Cute, sweet, endearing (NOT pity)
- **Usage:** "Ag shame!" when seeing something adorable
- **Foreign Confusion:** Sounds like feeling sorry
- **Cultural Context:** Positive exclamation
- **Examples:**
  - Baby animals: "Ag shame!"
  - Kind gesture: "Shame, that's sweet"

### The Quintessential Argentine Phrase

#### **"Che Boludo"**
- **"Che":**

  - Attention-getter: "Hey"
  - Associated with Che Guevara (named for constantly saying "che")
- **"Boludo":**

  - Actual Usage: "Dude," "man," "idiot" (context-dependent)
  - **Can be:**
    - Affectionate: "Che boludo, how are you?"
    - Insulting: "Boludo!" (angry tone)
    - Casual: Like "dude" in English
- **Combined:** "Che Boludo" = quintessentially Argentine
- **Usage Situations:**
  - Expressing surprise
  - Showing annoyance
  - Displaying affection

## China 🇨🇳

### Chinese Internet Censorship Memes

#### **Grass Mud Horse (草泥马)**
- **Pinyin:** Cǎo ní mǎ
- **Sounds Like:** "Fuck your mother" (肏你妈)
- **Actual Meaning:** "Grass mud horse" (literally)
- **Origin:** 2009, Baidu Baike (Chinese Wikipedia)
- **Purpose:** Circumvent censorship filters
- **Cultural Significance:** Protest against keyword filtering

**The 10 Mythical Creatures:**
- **Baidu 10 Mythical Creatures:** Series of fake articles
- **Method:** Names sound like profanities when spoken
- **Written Form:** Innocuous Chinese characters
- **Spoken Form:** Obvious profanities
- **Examples:**
  - 草泥马 (Grass Mud Horse) = 肏你妈
  - 法克鱿 (Fǎ Kè Yóu) = "Fuck you"
  - 雅蠛蝶 (Yǎ Miè Dié) = Japanese for "stop it" (yamete)
- **Spread:** Chinese forums, Baidu Tieba, widely discussed
- **Interpretation:** Satire and ridicule of censorship
- **Government Response:** Couldn't ban without banning own "harmonious society" language

#### **River Crab (河蟹)**
- **Pinyin:** Héxiè
- **Sounds Like:** "Harmonious" (和谐, héxié)
- **Origin:** Hu Jintao's "Harmonious Society" ideology (2004)
- **Evolution:**
  1. Government promotes "harmonious society"
  2. Used to justify censorship
  3. Netizens use "harmonious" as euphemism for censorship
  4. "Harmonious" gets censored
  5. Netizens switch to "river crab" (near homophone)
- **Usage:**
  - **"被和谐了"** (bèi héxié le) - "It has been harmonized" = censored
  - **"被河蟹了"** (bèi héxiè le) - "It has been river-crabbed" = censored
- **Cultural Significance:** Sarcastic defiance of official discourse
- **Irony:** Officials can't ban it without outlawing their own "harmonious society"

### Chinese Number Slang

- **Meaning:** "Awesome," "cool," "amazing," "smooth"

- **Represents:** 溜溜溜 (liùliùliù) - smooth, slick, proficient

- **Usage:** Complimenting impressive skill
- **Gaming Context:** Type "666" in chat after skilled play
- **Spread:** From gaming to general internet slang

#### **233 (èr sān sān)**

- **Represents:** 哈哈哈 (hāhāhā) - laughter

- **Cultural Equivalent:** Similar to "LMAO" in English
- **Still Popular:** Widely used despite platform's decline

### Thai Laughter Culture

- **Pronunciation:** Five in Thai = "ha" (ห้า)
