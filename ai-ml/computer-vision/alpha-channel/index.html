Alpha channel on a 3D model

A colour has three numbers (red, green, blue). The alpha channel is a fourth: how much of the surface is there, from 0 (nothing) to 1 (fully solid). It does not change the colour. It tells the renderer how much of that colour to put on screen, and how much of whatever is behind to keep. Drag the model to turn it, then change how the renderer treats alpha.

Where alpha lives on a model

In a textureRGBA image files (PNG, TGA, WebP). Alpha can vary across the surface: leaves, fences, hair cards, decals. Most alpha in games is here.
In the materialOne opacity number for the whole surface, such as tinted glass. In glTF this is the fourth value of baseColorFactor.
In vertex coloursEach vertex carries an alpha that is interpolated across the triangle, used for soft fades on particles and decals.

Three ways to draw it

Mode (glTF name)What happensGood forWatch out for
Opaque (OPAQUE)Alpha is ignored.Solid objectsA texture that has holes shows whatever colour sits under them.
Cutout (MASK)Pixels below the cutoff (default 0.5) are discarded, the rest are solid.Foliage, fences, chain-linkJagged edges; thin leaves can shimmer or vanish at a distance unless mipmaps are adjusted.
Blend (BLEND)The surface is mixed with what is behind it: result = colour × α + behind × (1 − α).Glass, smoke, ghosts, soft edgesOrder matters: sort back to front, and objects that cross each other cannot always be sorted.

Things that commonly go wrong

SortingBlending is not commutative: red over blue is not blue over red. If transparent things are drawn in the wrong order, a far pane hides a near one. Opaque things go first, then transparent ones far to near.
Straight vs premultipliedStraight alpha stores the colour unscaled and multiplies on blending. Premultiplied stores colour × α already and blends as colour + behind × (1 − α). Mixing the two up gives dark or bright fringes.
Edge fringesPixels at a cutout edge may keep the colour of the (often white or black) background they were painted over, so a leaf shows a halo. Artists extend the colour past the edge to avoid it.
MipmapsShrinking a texture averages alpha, so cutouts get fainter in the distance. Engines rescale alpha per level to keep the covered area constant.
