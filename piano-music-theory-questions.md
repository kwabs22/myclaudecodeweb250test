# 50 Piano Music Theory Questions

A comprehensive set of questions to test and reinforce your understanding of music theory concepts as they apply to piano playing and programming piano parts in a DAW.

---

## Basic Piano Knowledge (Questions 1-10)

### 1. How many keys does a standard piano have?
**Answer:** 88 keys (52 white keys and 36 black keys)

### 2. What is the name of the white key immediately to the left of any group of two black keys?
**Answer:** C (the "landmark" note for finding your position on the keyboard)

### 3. What is Middle C in scientific pitch notation, and what is its MIDI note number?
**Answer:** C4 in scientific pitch notation, MIDI note 60

### 4. What pattern of black keys helps you locate the note F on the piano?
**Answer:** F is immediately to the left of the group of three black keys

### 5. How many octaves does a standard 88-key piano span?
**Answer:** Just over 7 octaves (A0 to C8)

### 6. What is the interval between any white key and the black key immediately to its right?
**Answer:** One semitone (half step) - the black key is the sharp of the white key

### 7. Which two pairs of white keys have no black key between them?
**Answer:** E-F and B-C (these are natural semitones)

### 8. What is the typical playing range for most pop and rock piano parts?
**Answer:** C3 to C6 (three octaves centered around middle C)

### 9. If you play C and E together, how many semitones apart are they?
**Answer:** 4 semitones (a major 3rd)

### 10. What are the white keys on a piano named in order?
**Answer:** C-D-E-F-G-A-B, then the pattern repeats

## Piano Scales and Theory (Questions 11-20)

### 11. What is the fingering pattern for a C major scale in the right hand?
**Answer:** 1-2-3-1-2-3-4-5 (thumb crosses under after finger 3)

### 12. How many sharps or flats are in the key of C major?
**Answer:** Zero - C major has no sharps or flats (all white keys)

### 13. What is the relative minor of C major?
**Answer:** A minor (shares the same key signature, starts on the 6th degree)

### 14. In the key of G major, which note is sharped?
**Answer:** F# (the 7th degree of the scale)

### 15. What scale uses only the black keys on the piano?
**Answer:** F# major pentatonic (or Gb major pentatonic) - also the enharmonic Db major pentatonic

### 16. What is the formula for the harmonic minor scale in whole and half steps?
**Answer:** W-H-W-W-H-W+H-H (whole-half-whole-whole-half-whole&half-half)

### 17. If you start on D and play only white keys, what mode are you playing?
**Answer:** D Dorian (the 2nd mode of C major)

### 18. What interval is created by playing C and G together?
**Answer:** Perfect 5th (7 semitones)

### 19. How many notes are in a pentatonic scale?
**Answer:** 5 notes (penta = five)

### 20. In a chromatic scale starting on C, what is the 7th note?
**Answer:** F# or Gb (C-C#-D-D#-E-F-F#...)

## Piano Chords and Voicings (Questions 21-30)

### 21. What three notes make up a C major triad in root position?
**Answer:** C-E-G (root, major 3rd, perfect 5th)

### 22. What is the difference between a C major chord and a C minor chord?
**Answer:** The 3rd - C major has E (major 3rd), C minor has Eb (minor 3rd)

### 23. What four notes comprise a Cmaj7 chord?
**Answer:** C-E-G-B (root, major 3rd, perfect 5th, major 7th)

### 24. What is a "shell voicing" for a C7 chord?
**Answer:** Playing just the essential tones: C (root), E (3rd), and Bb (7th), often omitting the 5th

### 25. If you play C-F-G, what type of chord is this?
**Answer:** Csus4 (suspended 4th chord - replaces the 3rd with the 4th)

### 26. In a first inversion C major chord, which note is in the bass?
**Answer:** E (the 3rd becomes the lowest note: E-G-C)

### 27. What is a "10th" interval, and why is it important in piano voicing?
**Answer:** An octave plus a 3rd (17 semitones); important because it creates fuller sound than a 3rd when voiced in bass register

### 28. What notes would you play for a rootless Cmaj9 voicing (common in jazz)?
**Answer:** E-G-B-D (3rd, 5th, 7th, 9th - omitting the root C)

### 29. What is the difference between a rolled chord and a blocked chord?
**Answer:** Rolled = notes played in quick succession (arpeggiated); Blocked = all notes played simultaneously

### 30. In stride piano, what does the left hand typically play on beats 1 and 3?
**Answer:** Bass notes (root or root and 5th of the chord)

## Piano Technique in Piano Roll (Questions 31-40)

### 31. When programming an Alberti bass pattern in C major, what is the note order?
**Answer:** C-G-E-G (root-5th-3rd-5th, repeated)

### 32. What MIDI CC number controls the sustain pedal?
**Answer:** CC64 (values 0-63 = off, 64-127 = on)

### 33. What velocity range represents piano (soft) playing?
**Answer:** Approximately 40-60 (pp to p dynamic range)

### 34. How should you program a realistic glissando in piano roll?
**Answer:** Rapid succession of notes (white or black keys) with slight timing offset (2-5ms apart) and increasing velocity

### 35. For a Chopin-style waltz accompaniment in 3/4 time, what is the left hand pattern?
**Answer:** Bass note on beat 1, chord on beats 2 and 3

### 36. When programming a piano tremolo, what note duration should you use?
**Answer:** Very short notes (1/32 or 1/64) repeated rapidly, with slight velocity variation

### 37. How do you create a realistic rolled chord ascending in piano roll?
**Answer:** Offset each note by 5-15ms, starting with lowest note, ending with highest

### 38. What velocity value typically represents fortissimo (very loud) playing?
**Answer:** 100-127 (ff to fff dynamic range)

### 39. For authentic two-hand independence, what should you avoid in piano roll programming?
**Answer:** Impossible stretches (more than an octave per hand), overlapping hands in impossible ways, or patterns that require more than 10 fingers

### 40. How should sustain pedal be used when changing chords to avoid muddiness?
**Answer:** Release pedal briefly during chord changes (syncopated pedaling), then re-engage after new chord starts

## Applied Piano Music Theory (Questions 41-50)

### 41. In a ii-V-I progression in C major, what are the three chords?
**Answer:** Dm7 (ii) - G7 (V) - Cmaj7 (I)

### 42. What is the purpose of using a Vsus4 to V resolution?
**Answer:** Creates tension and release; the 4th resolves down to the 3rd, strengthening the pull to the I chord

### 43. In stride piano, what interval is typically played on beat 2 of the left hand?
**Answer:** A 10th (octave + 3rd) or sometimes just a chord

### 44. What is the I-V-vi-IV progression in the key of F major?
**Answer:** F - C - Dm - Bb

### 45. Why should you avoid playing small intervals (2nds, 3rds) in the bass register (below C3)?
**Answer:** They create muddy, unclear sound due to low frequency overtones clashing

### 46. What is "comping" in jazz piano?
**Answer:** Rhythmic, syncopated chord accompaniment that supports a soloist, typically using shell voicings on off-beats

### 47. In gospel piano, where do runs typically occur in relation to the beat?
**Answer:** Leading into the downbeat (usually on beat 4, resolving to beat 1)

### 48. What is the characteristic of a Lydian mode that makes it sound "dreamy"?
**Answer:** The raised 4th (#4 or #11), which creates an augmented 4th from the root

### 49. When using parallel motion (planing), why does it work despite breaking traditional voice leading rules?
**Answer:** Creates impressionistic, colorful sound; uniform motion prevents individual voices from standing out, creating a cohesive harmonic wash

### 50. In a montuno pattern (Latin piano), which hand typically plays the repetitive syncopated figure?
**Answer:** Right hand (while left hand plays bass tumbao pattern)

## Bonus: Advanced Concepts (5 Challenge Questions)

### 51. What is the difference between sostenuto pedal (CC66) and sustain pedal (CC64)?
**Answer:** Sostenuto sustains only notes already being held when pedal is pressed; sustain pedal sustains all notes played while pedal is down

### 52. In a diminished 7th chord, what is the interval between each consecutive note?
**Answer:** Minor 3rd (3 semitones) - creates a symmetrical chord structure

### 53. What makes a "Neapolitan sixth" chord unique in classical harmony?
**Answer:** It's a major chord built on the lowered 2nd scale degree, typically used in first inversion

### 54. How do you voice a quartal chord, and what genre commonly uses it?
**Answer:** Stack notes in 4ths (e.g., C-F-Bb-Eb); common in jazz and modern classical music

### 55. What is the relationship between C Lydian and G major scale?
**Answer:** They share the same notes - C Lydian is the 4th mode of G major

1. **Use the piano roll visually:** See the patterns and relationships between notes
2. **Listen actively:** Program these concepts in your DAW and hear the differences
3. **Practice both hands separately:** Understand left-hand vs. right-hand roles
4. **Study real piano performances:** Analyze how pianists use dynamics and pedaling
5. **Experiment with voicings:** Try different inversions and registers

## Practical Application Exercises

### Exercise 1: Voicing Comparison
Program the same progression (I-IV-V-I in C) using:
- Block chords in root position
- Shell voicings
- Rolled chords with 10ths in left hand

### Exercise 2: Dynamics Challenge
Create a 16-bar piano piece that demonstrates:
- pp (pianissimo) - velocity 40-50
- mf (mezzo-forte) - velocity 75-85
- ff (fortissimo) - velocity 110-127

### Exercise 3: Style Imitation
Program 8 bars each of:
- Classical waltz (3/4, Chopin-style accompaniment)
- Jazz comping (4/4, syncopated shell voicings)
- Gospel runs (connecting chord changes)
- Latin montuno (2-bar repeated pattern)

### Exercise 4: Left Hand Patterns
Create bass line variations:
- Alberti bass (C-G-E-G pattern)
- Stride bass (root on 1 & 3, chord on 2 & 4)
- Walking bass (stepwise quarter notes)
- Arpegiated bass (broken chord pattern)

### Exercise 5: Pedaling Study
Program the same chord progression:
- Without sustain pedal (staccato)
- With full sustain pedal (legato)
- With syncopated pedaling (changing pedal with chords)
- With half-pedaling (CC64 value 40-60)
