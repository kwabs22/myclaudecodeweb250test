Geometry you can drag

Orange dots are handles: drag them (mouse or finger) and every number updates. Each section shows the arithmetic behind the picture, and the code in geometry.js is checked against slower reference methods by check.js.

1. A triangle: area, angles, which way round

Three points make a triangle. Half of the cross product of two sides is its area, and the sign of that cross product tells you whether the points go anticlockwise (positive) or clockwise (negative).

2. Do two segments cross?

Each end of one segment is tested against the line through the other: orient is positive on the left, negative on the right, zero on the line. Two segments cross when each one's ends are on opposite sides of the other.

3. Is the point inside the shape?

Two rules. Ray casting: shoot a ray to the right and count the edges it crosses (odd means inside). Winding number: count how many times the outline goes round the point (not zero means inside). Drag the corners until the outline crosses itself to see them disagree.

4. The convex hull: a rubber band round the points

The hull is the smallest convex shape holding every point. The fast method sorts the points and keeps only left turns. The page also runs the slow method (a point is a corner unless some other points enclose it) and says whether they agree.

5. Transformations are matrices

Turning, stretching and shearing a shape are each a 2×2 matrix, and doing one after another multiplies the matrices. The determinant is how much the area changes, and a negative one means the shape has been flipped over.

6. 3D turns and a pinhole camera

A cube is turned by a 3×3 rotation matrix, then a pinhole camera divides by distance: a point at depth d in front of the camera (the camera looks down −Z, as in three.js) lands at u = f·X/d + cx, v = cy − f·Y/d.
