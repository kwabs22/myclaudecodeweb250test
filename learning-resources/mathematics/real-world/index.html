Combinatorics in the real world

Combinatorics is the mathematics of counting arrangements and choices. Here are 53 everyday problems, grouped by the question being asked of them: how many, what is likely, can we try them all, which is best. Prediction is one group: when outcomes are equally likely, a forecast is a count of what you care about divided by a count of everything that could happen. Pick a dot, change the sliders, and the numbers are recomputed live.

Solo or group? Each problem is also marked by who is involved. A solo task is one person or system facing the problem alone: their ticket, hand, route or data. A group task depends on how several people or agents fit, meet, share or compete: a team, a league, a crowd. Solo tasks grow with the number of options; group tasks also grow with the number of people, usually much faster (pairs grow with n², sub-groups with 2n, line-ups with n!). Use the buttons to see one kind at a time.

Starting a hobby group, step by step

Starting a hobby group runs into the same counting questions in a fairly fixed order. Each step below opens the task that answers it; the ones marked hobby are the tasks that make up this path, and the Hobby groups button above highlights them on the map and in the list.

    The logic is in tasks.js and is checked by check.js (run node check.js): the poker counts against all 2,598,960 hands, the route and packing answers against brute force, the seating, team-split and Secret Santa counts against exhaustive enumeration, and so on. Related questions are in the 100 combinatorics questions, and the matching code tasks are mapped in the mathematics README.
