## Basic Music Fundamentals

### 1. The Piano Roll Interface
**Concept:** The piano roll is a vertical piano keyboard on the left (showing pitch) and a horizontal grid (showing time). Higher notes are at the top, lower notes at the bottom.
**Piano Roll Application:** Each row represents a different pitch. The grid shows when notes play and for how long.
**Practical Tip:** Zoom in for detailed note editing; zoom out for overall composition view.

### 2. Notes and the Musical Alphabet
**Concept:** Music uses 12 notes: C, C#, D, D#, E, F, F#, G, G#, A, A#, B, then repeats.
**Piano Roll Application:** White keys (C, D, E, F, G, A, B) alternate with black keys (sharps/flats).
**Practical Tip:** Enable piano roll highlighting to show C notes or root notes of your key.

### 3. Octaves
**Concept:** An octave is the distance between one note and the next occurrence of the same note (12 semitones). Middle C is typically C4.
**Piano Roll Application:** Moving up or down 12 rows in the piano roll equals one octave.
**Practical Tip:** Double melodies an octave apart for thickness; bass typically sits in C1-C3.

### 4. Semitones and Whole Tones
**Concept:** A semitone (half-step) is the smallest interval in Western music (1 key). A whole tone (whole-step) equals 2 semitones.
**Piano Roll Application:** One row up/down = 1 semitone. Two rows up/down = 1 whole tone.
**Practical Tip:** Use semitone movement for smooth, chromatic transitions between notes.

### 5. Accidentals (Sharps and Flats)
**Concept:** Sharp (♯) raises a note by one semitone. Flat (♭) lowers by one semitone. C# and D♭ are enharmonic (same pitch, different names).
**Piano Roll Application:** Black keys can be named as either sharp or flat (e.g., C# = D♭).
**Practical Tip:** Most DAWs display sharps by default; context determines whether to think sharp or flat.

## Rhythm and Time

### 6. Note Duration and Length
**Concept:** Notes have durations: whole note (4 beats), half note (2 beats), quarter note (1 beat), eighth note (0.5 beats), sixteenth note (0.25 beats).
**Piano Roll Application:** Note length is shown by the horizontal width of the note block.
**Practical Tip:** Drag note endpoints to adjust length; press notes create staccato, longer notes create legato.

### 7. Time Signatures
**Concept:** Time signature (e.g., 4/4, 3/4, 6/8) indicates beats per measure and which note value gets the beat.
**Piano Roll Application:** Vertical lines mark bar divisions. 4/4 = 4 quarter notes per bar.
**Practical Tip:** Most popular music uses 4/4. Experiment with 3/4 for waltzes, 6/8 for ballads, 7/8 for progressive sounds.

### 8. Tempo (BPM)
**Concept:** Tempo is the speed of music measured in Beats Per Minute (BPM). 120 BPM = 120 quarter note beats in one minute.
**Piano Roll Application:** The grid spacing represents time divisions based on project tempo.
**Practical Tip:** Hip-hop: 80-100 BPM, House: 120-130 BPM, Drum & Bass: 160-180 BPM, Dubstep: 140 BPM (half-time feel).

### 9. Grid Quantization
**Concept:** Quantization snaps notes to the nearest grid division (1/4, 1/8, 1/16, etc.).
**Piano Roll Application:** Grid lines help align notes rhythmically.
**Practical Tip:** Use 1/16 quantization for detailed work, but humanize by slightly shifting notes off-grid for natural feel.

### 10. Triplets
**Concept:** Triplets divide a beat into three equal parts instead of two.
**Piano Roll Application:** Set grid to triplet mode (usually 1/6, 1/12, or 1/24 divisions).
**Practical Tip:** Triplets create a swung, bouncy feel. Common in shuffle rhythms and blues.

## Intervals

### 11. Interval Basics
**Concept:** An interval is the distance between two notes. Intervals are counted by number (2nd, 3rd, 4th, etc.) and quality (major, minor, perfect).
**Piano Roll Application:** Count rows between notes to determine interval (e.g., 4 semitones = major 3rd).
**Practical Tip:** Learn interval sounds by ear to quickly create melodic relationships.

### 12. Perfect Intervals
**Concept:** Perfect intervals include unison (0), 4th (5 semitones), 5th (7 semitones), and octave (12 semitones).
**Piano Roll Application:**
- Perfect 4th: 5 rows up (C to F)
- Perfect 5th: 7 rows up (C to G)
**Practical Tip:** Perfect 5ths sound powerful and stable; use for power chords and strong bass lines.

### 13. Major and Minor Intervals
**Concept:** Major intervals (2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th) are bright; minor intervals are darker, one semitone smaller.
**Piano Roll Application:**
- Major 3rd: 4 rows (C to E) - happy sound
- Minor 3rd: 3 rows (C to E♭) - sad sound
**Practical Tip:** The 3rd determines if a chord sounds major (happy) or minor (sad).

### 14. Tritone
**Concept:** The tritone (augmented 4th/diminished 5th) is 6 semitones, historically called "diabolus in musica" (devil in music).
**Piano Roll Application:** Exactly 6 rows (half an octave) - e.g., C to F#/G♭.
**Practical Tip:** Creates tension and dissonance; resolve it to create satisfying harmonic movement.

### 15. Consonance vs Dissonance
**Concept:** Consonant intervals sound stable and pleasant (3rds, 5ths, 6ths). Dissonant intervals create tension (2nds, 7ths, tritones).
**Piano Roll Application:** Place notes closer together (1-2 rows) for dissonance, wider for consonance.
**Practical Tip:** Use dissonance to create tension, resolve to consonance for release.

## Scales and Modes

### 16. Major Scale
**Concept:** The major scale formula: W-W-H-W-W-W-H (W=whole step, H=half step). C major: C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C.
**Piano Roll Application:** In C major, only use white keys. Pattern: 2-2-1-2-2-2-1 semitones.
**Practical Tip:** Enable scale highlighting in piano roll to only show notes in your chosen scale.

### 17. Natural Minor Scale
**Concept:** Natural minor formula: W-H-W-W-H-W-W. A minor: A-B-C-D-E-F-G-A (all white keys).
**Piano Roll Application:** Pattern: 2-1-2-2-1-2-2 semitones. The "relative minor" shares notes with its relative major.
**Practical Tip:** A minor is the relative minor of C major. Start on the 6th degree of major scale for relative minor.

### 18. Harmonic Minor Scale
**Concept:** Natural minor with raised 7th degree. Creates exotic, classical sound.
**Piano Roll Application:** A harmonic minor: A-B-C-D-E-F-G#-A. Notice the 3-semitone gap (F to G#).
**Practical Tip:** The raised 7th creates strong pull to the tonic. Common in metal, classical, and Middle Eastern music.

### 19. Melodic Minor Scale
**Concept:** Ascending: natural minor with raised 6th and 7th. Descending: natural minor.
**Piano Roll Application:** Ascending A melodic minor: A-B-C-D-E-F#-G#-A.
**Practical Tip:** Creates smooth melodic motion. Jazz musicians often use ascending pattern for both directions.

### 20. Pentatonic Scales
**Concept:** 5-note scales. Major pentatonic: 1-2-3-5-6. Minor pentatonic: 1-♭3-4-5-♭7.
**Piano Roll Application:**
- C major pentatonic: C-D-E-G-A (remove 4th and 7th from major scale)
- A minor pentatonic: A-C-D-E-G (remove 2nd and 6th from minor scale)
**Practical Tip:** Nearly impossible to play "wrong" notes. Perfect for improvisation, blues, rock, and pop.

### 21. Blues Scale
**Concept:** Minor pentatonic + ♭5 (blue note). Creates bluesy, soulful sound.
**Piano Roll Application:** A blues scale: A-C-D-D#-E-G. The D# is the blue note.
**Practical Tip:** Bend into the blue note or use it as a passing tone for authentic blues feel.

### 22. Modes of the Major Scale
**Concept:** Seven modes created by starting major scale on different degrees:
- Ionian (major): bright, happy
- Dorian: minor with raised 6th (jazzy)
- Phrygian: minor with ♭2 (Spanish/dark)
- Lydian: major with #4 (dreamy, ethereal)
- Mixolydian: major with ♭7 (bluesy, dominant)
- Aeolian (natural minor): sad, melancholic
- Locrian: diminished (unstable, rarely used)

**Piano Roll Application:** D Dorian uses same notes as C major but starts/ends on D.
**Practical Tip:** Dorian for funk/jazz, Phrygian for metal/Spanish, Lydian for film scores, Mixolydian for rock.

### 23. Chromatic Scale
**Concept:** All 12 notes: every semitone. No tonal center.
**Piano Roll Application:** Every single row/key, all colors included.
**Practical Tip:** Use chromaticism for runs, transitions, or jazz improvisation. Creates tension.

### 24. Whole Tone Scale
**Concept:** Scale built entirely of whole steps (2 semitones). C whole tone: C-D-E-F#-G#-A#-C.
**Piano Roll Application:** Skip every other note: 2-2-2-2-2-2 pattern.
**Practical Tip:** Creates dreamy, floating, ambiguous sound. No perfect 5th = no strong tonal center. Used by Debussy.

### 25. Diminished Scales
**Concept:** Symmetrical scales alternating half and whole steps. Two types: half-whole and whole-half.
**Piano Roll Application:** C half-whole diminished: C-Db-Eb-E-F#-G-A-Bb-C (1-2-1-2-1-2-1-2).
**Practical Tip:** Creates tension over diminished chords. Common in jazz and modern classical.

## Chords

### 26. Triads (3-Note Chords)
**Concept:** Triads have three notes: root, 3rd, and 5th.
- Major triad: root, major 3rd, perfect 5th (happy)
- Minor triad: root, minor 3rd, perfect 5th (sad)
- Diminished triad: root, minor 3rd, diminished 5th (tense)
- Augmented triad: root, major 3rd, augmented 5th (unstable)

**Piano Roll Application:**
- C major: C-E-G (0-4-7 semitones)
- C minor: C-Eb-G (0-3-7 semitones)
- C diminished: C-Eb-Gb (0-3-6 semitones)
- C augmented: C-E-G# (0-4-8 semitones)

**Practical Tip:** Stack notes vertically in piano roll. Align all chord notes to start at same time for chord stabs.

### 27. Seventh Chords
**Concept:** Triads + 7th interval. Adds color and complexity.
- Major 7th (Cmaj7): C-E-G-B (jazz, dreamy)
- Dominant 7th (C7): C-E-G-Bb (blues, resolution)
- Minor 7th (Cm7): C-Eb-G-Bb (smooth, jazzy)
- Half-diminished 7th (Cm7b5): C-Eb-Gb-Bb (tense)
- Diminished 7th (Cdim7): C-Eb-Gb-Bbb/A (very tense)

**Piano Roll Application:** Add a note 10 (minor 7th) or 11 (major 7th) semitones above root.
**Practical Tip:** Maj7 for lush chords, Dom7 for blues/funk, m7 for smooth progressions.

### 28. Extended Chords (9th, 11th, 13th)
**Concept:** Chords with notes beyond the 7th.
- 9th = 2nd + octave (adds brightness)
- 11th = 4th + octave (can clash with 3rd)
- 13th = 6th + octave (lush, sophisticated)

**Piano Roll Application:** Stack more notes: Cmaj9 = C-E-G-B-D (root, 3, 5, 7, 9).
**Practical Tip:** Don't play all notes - voice chords by omitting 5th or root. Common in jazz, neo-soul, and lo-fi.

### 29. Suspended Chords
**Concept:** Replace 3rd with 2nd or 4th.
- Sus2: root, 2nd, 5th (C-D-G) - open, modern
- Sus4: root, 4th, 5th (C-F-G) - tension wanting resolution

**Piano Roll Application:** Csus4 = C-F-G (0-5-7 semitones). Csus2 = C-D-G (0-2-7 semitones).
**Practical Tip:** Sus4 creates tension that resolves beautifully to major or minor. Common in pop and rock.

### 30. Power Chords
**Concept:** Root + perfect 5th (+ octave). No 3rd = neither major nor minor.
**Piano Roll Application:** C5 = C-G or C-G-C (0-7 or 0-7-12 semitones).
**Practical Tip:** Essential for rock and metal. Sounds powerful with distortion. No 3rd means works in any context.

## Harmonic Progressions

### 31. Diatonic Chord Progression
**Concept:** Chords built from notes within a scale. In major: I-ii-iii-IV-V-vi-vii°
- I, IV, V = major chords
- ii, iii, vi = minor chords
- vii° = diminished chord

**Piano Roll Application:** In C major:
- I = C major, ii = D minor, iii = E minor, IV = F major, V = G major, vi = A minor, vii° = B diminished

**Practical Tip:** Roman numerals show chord function. Uppercase = major, lowercase = minor.

### 32. Common Progressions in Major
**Concept:** Familiar chord sequences:
- I-V-vi-IV (pop progression): C-G-Am-F
- I-IV-V (blues/rock): C-F-G
- ii-V-I (jazz): Dm-G-C
- I-vi-IV-V (50s progression): C-Am-F-G

**Piano Roll Application:** Create progression first, then add melody on top.
**Practical Tip:** The I-V-vi-IV progression is used in countless hits. Experiment with different orderings.

### 33. Common Progressions in Minor
**Concept:**
- i-VI-III-VII (epic progression): Am-F-C-G
- i-iv-v (minor blues): Am-Dm-Em
- i-VII-VI-VII (rock): Am-G-F-G

**Piano Roll Application:** Natural minor uses natural minor scale chords.
**Practical Tip:** Harmonic minor's V chord is major (E major in A minor), creating stronger resolution to i.

### 34. The Circle of Fifths
**Concept:** Circle showing relationships between keys. Moving clockwise = up a 5th. Shows which keys are closely related.
**Piano Roll Application:** Modulate to adjacent keys (1 accidental difference) for smooth key changes.
**Practical Tip:** Root movement by 5ths (or 4ths) creates strong harmonic motion. ii-V-I uses descending 5ths.

### 35. Cadences
**Concept:** Harmonic endings/pauses:
- Authentic cadence: V-I (strong ending)
- Plagal cadence: IV-I ("Amen" cadence)
- Half cadence: ends on V (question mark)
- Deceptive cadence: V-vi (surprise)

**Piano Roll Application:** Place cadences at phrase endings (typically every 4 or 8 bars).
**Practical Tip:** V-I is the strongest resolution. Use at song end. Half cadences maintain momentum.

## Melody Writing

### 36. Stepwise Motion vs. Leaps
**Concept:** Melodies move by steps (1-2 semitones) or leaps (3+ semitones). Good melodies balance both.
**Piano Roll Application:** Stepwise motion = adjacent or near-adjacent rows. Leaps = larger vertical jumps.
**Practical Tip:** After a leap, resolve by stepwise motion in opposite direction. This creates natural melodic contour.

### 37. Melodic Contour
**Concept:** The shape of melody over time: ascending, descending, arch (up then down), or valley (down then up).
**Piano Roll Application:** Visualize melody shape by note heights. Arch contours are very common.
**Practical Tip:** Verse melodies often stay in narrow range; chorus melodies leap to higher notes for impact.

### 38. Call and Response
**Concept:** Musical "question and answer" - a phrase (call) followed by a response phrase (answer).
**Piano Roll Application:** 2-bar call phrase, then 2-bar response. Response often ends on a more resolved note.
**Practical Tip:** Call might end on unstable note (2nd, 7th), response on stable note (root, 3rd, 5th).

### 39. Motifs and Themes
**Concept:** A motif is a short, memorable musical idea. Themes are built by developing motifs.
**Piano Roll Application:** Create 2-4 note pattern, then repeat/vary it throughout the melody.
**Practical Tip:** Variation techniques: transpose, invert, retrograde (reverse), augment (lengthen), diminish (shorten).

### 40. Approach Notes
**Concept:** Notes that "approach" target chord tones from above or below:
- Chromatic approach: half-step away
- Diatonic approach: scale step away

**Piano Roll Application:** Place approach note just before target note (1 or 2 semitones away).
**Practical Tip:** Approach notes add sophistication to melodies. Common in jazz and R&B.

## Voice Leading and Harmony

### 41. Voice Leading Basics
**Concept:** The art of moving smoothly from one chord to another by minimal note movement.
**Piano Roll Application:** When changing chords, move each note to nearest note in next chord.
**Practical Tip:** Good voice leading creates smooth, professional-sounding progressions. Avoid large jumps in all voices.

### 42. Chord Inversions
**Concept:** Reordering chord notes so root isn't the lowest note.
- Root position: root on bottom (C-E-G)
- First inversion: 3rd on bottom (E-G-C)
- Second inversion: 5th on bottom (G-C-E)

**Piano Roll Application:** Keep some notes the same, move others to create inversion.
**Practical Tip:** Inversions create smoother bass lines and voice leading. Bass note doesn't always need to be the root.

### 43. Harmonization in 3rds and 6ths
**Concept:** Doubling melody with notes a 3rd (4 semitones) or 6th (9 semitones) above/below.
**Piano Roll Application:** Copy melody, shift up/down 3-4 semitones (3rd) or 8-9 semitones (6th).
**Practical Tip:** Parallel 3rds = classic sweet sound (country, pop). Parallel 6ths = fuller, richer sound.

### 44. Counterpoint Basics
**Concept:** Multiple independent melodic lines played simultaneously that follow specific rules.
**Piano Roll Application:** Create a second melody that complements main melody rhythmically and harmonically.
**Practical Tip:** When one melody moves, other stays still or moves opposite direction. Avoid parallel 5ths/octaves.

### 45. Bass Line Construction
**Concept:** Bass notes outline harmony and provide rhythmic foundation.
**Piano Roll Application:** Use chord roots (or chord tones) in lower octaves (C1-C3). Add rhythmic patterns.
**Practical Tip:** Techniques: root-fifth pattern, walking bass (stepwise motion), arpeggiated bass, pedal tone (sustained note).

## Rhythm Techniques

### 46. Syncopation
**Concept:** Emphasizing off-beats or weak beats. Creates groove and rhythmic interest.
**Piano Roll Application:** Place notes between main beat divisions (off the quarter note grid).
**Practical Tip:** Essential for funk, reggae, and jazz. Anticipate downbeats by placing notes slightly early.

### 47. Polyrhythm
**Concept:** Two or more contrasting rhythms played simultaneously (e.g., 3 against 4).
**Piano Roll Application:** Layer patterns with different grid settings. One part in triplets, another in straight time.
**Practical Tip:** 3:2 polyrhythm (triplets vs duplets) adds complexity. Common in African music and progressive rock.

### 48. Swing and Shuffle
**Concept:** Uneven eighth notes - first note longer, second shorter (triplet feel).
**Piano Roll Application:** Move every second eighth note later in time (towards next beat).
**Practical Tip:** Most DAWs have swing quantization. Amount typically 50-75%. Essential for jazz, blues, and hip-hop.

### 49. Rests and Negative Space
**Concept:** Silence is as important as sound. Rests create breathing room and tension.
**Piano Roll Application:** Leave gaps between notes instead of filling all space.
**Practical Tip:** Less is more. Strategic silence makes notes that do play more impactful. Essential for groove.

### 50. Velocity and Dynamics
**Concept:** Velocity controls how hard a note is "hit" - affects volume and timbre. Dynamics create expression.
**Piano Roll Application:** Adjust note velocity (usually 0-127). Lower = softer, higher = louder.
**Practical Tip:** Humanize MIDI by varying velocities. Emphasize beats 1 & 3 or 2 & 4. Avoid all notes at max velocity (127).

## Advanced Concepts

### 51. Modal Interchange (Bonus Concept)
**Concept:** Borrowing chords from parallel modes/keys (e.g., using minor iv in major key).
**Piano Roll Application:** In C major, use chords from C minor: Fm, Ab, Bb.
**Practical Tip:** iv chord in major (C-Fm) = emotional, Beatles-esque sound. Common in pop and rock.

## Guitar-Specific Learning

### 52. Guitar Fretboard Layout
**Concept:** Guitar strings (standard tuning, low to high): E-A-D-G-B-E. Each fret = one semitone.
**Piano Roll Application:** Translate guitar tabs to piano roll by knowing string pitches:
- 6th string (E): E2
- 5th string (A): A2
- 4th string (D): D3
- 3rd string (G): G3
- 2nd string (B): B3
- 1st string (E): E4

**Practical Tip:** When programming guitar parts in piano roll, think in terms of fret positions. Open strings are important for authentic voicings.

### 53. CAGED System
**Concept:** Five chord shapes that cover entire fretboard: C, A, G, E, D shapes. Same chord shape moves up/down neck.
**Piano Roll Application:** Understanding CAGED helps you voice chords like a guitarist would:
- E shape: root on 6th string (wider voicing)
- A shape: root on 5th string
- D shape: root on 4th string (no 5th string played)

**Practical Tip:** Program guitar chords by stacking notes as they appear in guitar voicings, not piano voicings. Guitar chords often skip strings.

### 54. Guitar Chord Voicings
**Concept:** Guitars voice chords differently than keyboards due to string tuning and hand limitations.
**Piano Roll Application:** Common guitar voicings:
- Open C major: C3-E3-G3-C4-E4 (spanning 2 octaves)
- Barre E shape F major: F2-C3-F3-A3-C4-F4
- Open G major: G2-B2-D3-G3-B3-G4

**Practical Tip:** Guitar voicings often double roots and omit 5ths. Use wider intervals (10ths instead of 3rds) for authentic guitar sound.

### 55. Power Chord Shapes on Guitar
**Concept:** Root + 5th (+ octave) played on adjacent strings. Foundation of rock/metal guitar.
**Piano Roll Application:**
- 6th string root: E2-B2-E3 (E5 power chord)
- 5th string root: A2-E3-A3 (A5 power chord)

**Practical Tip:** When programming distorted guitar, use just root and 5th in lower register. Add octave for thickness.

### 56. Guitar Scale Patterns (Box Positions)
**Concept:** Scales laid out in "boxes" or patterns on the fretboard. 5 positions for pentatonic, 7 for major/minor.
**Piano Roll Application:** Understanding positions helps create realistic guitar solos:
- Position 1 (Minor Pentatonic in A): mostly on frets 5-8
- Position 2: shifts up to frets 7-10

**Practical Tip:** Guitar players think vertically (across strings) not horizontally (along one string). Program melodies using 2-3 notes per string pattern.

### 57. String Bends
**Concept:** Pushing/pulling string to raise pitch (usually 1-2 semitones). Signature guitar technique.
**Piano Roll Application:** Use pitch bend automation or modulation wheel:
- Whole step bend: start on D, bend to E
- Half step bend: start on E, bend to F

**Practical Tip:** Pre-bend (bend before playing), bend and release, and vibrato (slight rapid bends) add realism to programmed guitar.

### 58. Hammer-Ons and Pull-Offs
**Concept:** Legato technique where notes are played without picking. Creates smooth, fluid sound.
**Piano Roll Application:**
- Hammer-on: first note higher velocity, second note lower velocity, no gap
- Pull-off: reverse direction, descending notes

**Practical Tip:** Reduce velocity (60-80) on hammered/pulled notes. Slightly overlap note timing for legato effect.

### 59. Guitar Strumming Patterns
**Concept:** Chords played in rhythmic patterns with up/down strokes. Each string struck slightly offset.
**Piano Roll Application:** Strum = notes don't start simultaneously:
- Downstroke: lowest notes first, ascending (3-5ms apart)
- Upstroke: highest notes first, descending

**Practical Tip:** Use negative note delay or humanize function. Downstrokes emphasize bass, upstrokes emphasize treble.

### 60. Alternate Tunings
**Concept:** Non-standard string tunings create different chord voicings and tones.
**Piano Roll Application:** Common alternate tunings:
- Drop D: D-A-D-G-B-E (6th string down to D2)
- Open G: D-G-D-G-B-D (open strings = G chord)
- DADGAD: D-A-D-G-A-D (folk/Celtic sound)

**Practical Tip:** When programming in alternate tunings, translate accordingly. Drop D gives access to lower D for heavy riffs.

### 61. Guitar-Friendly Keys
**Concept:** Keys with open strings are easier and ring better on guitar: E, A, D, G, C.
**Piano Roll Application:** Program in these keys for most authentic guitar sound. Open strings add resonance.
**Practical Tip:** E minor/major, A minor/major, and D minor/major are guitar favorites. Use open string notes for drones.

### 62. Two-Hand Tapping
**Concept:** Using both hands on fretboard to play rapid arpeggios and wide intervals.
**Piano Roll Application:** Create patterns with large interval jumps:
- Low note (picked): E2
- High note (tapped): G4
- Mid note (pulled-off): B3

**Practical Tip:** Velocities: picked note (100-127), tapped note (90-110), pulled notes (70-90).

### 63. Palm Muting
**Concept:** Muting strings with picking hand palm creates percussive, tight sound.
**Piano Roll Application:** Shorten note length dramatically (staccato) and reduce velocity slightly.
**Practical Tip:** Combine with power chords for classic metal rhythm. Notes should be 1/16 or shorter with dead space between.

### 64. Artificial Harmonics
**Concept:** Creating harmonic tones by lightly touching string at specific frets while picking.
**Piano Roll Application:** Play notes 1-2 octaves higher than fretted position. Add shimmer/bell tone.
**Practical Tip:** Most common at 12th fret (octave), 7th fret (octave + 5th), and 5th fret (two octaves).

### 65. Slide Guitar Techniques
**Concept:** Using slide (metal/glass tube) creates smooth glissando between notes.
**Piano Roll Application:** Use portamento/glide function. Notes slide continuously rather than step.
**Practical Tip:** Often played in open tunings. Slide vibrato = slight wobble at end of phrases. Popular in blues and country.

## Piano-Specific Learning

### 66. Piano Keyboard Layout
**Concept:** Piano has 88 keys. Pattern of 7 white + 5 black keys repeats each octave. C is always left of two-black-key group.
**Piano Roll Application:** Piano roll directly mirrors piano keyboard - easiest 1:1 translation.
**Practical Tip:** Middle C (C4) is center of piano. Most pop/rock piano sits in C3-C6 range.

### 67. Left Hand vs. Right Hand Roles
**Concept:** Typically: left hand plays bass/chords, right hand plays melody/top notes.
**Piano Roll Application:** Split programming:
- Left hand: C1-C4 (bass lines, chord roots, chord shells)
- Right hand: C4-C7 (melody, chord tops, runs)

**Practical Tip:** Think like a pianist - what can two hands physically play? Avoid programming impossible stretches (>octave span per hand).

### 68. Piano Chord Voicings
**Concept:** Piano voicings differ from guitar - more vertical stacking, use of 10ths (octave + 3rd).
**Piano Roll Application:** Root position vs shell voicings:
- Full voicing: C2-C3-E3-G3-C4 (left hand root, right hand triad)
- Shell voicing: C2-E3-Bb3 (C7 - root, 3rd, 7th only)

**Practical Tip:** Don't muddy low register - keep intervals wider below C3. Above C4, closer intervals sound clear.

### 69. Stride Piano
**Concept:** Left hand alternates bass notes (1-2 beats) with chords (3-4 beats). Classic jazz/ragtime style.
**Piano Roll Application:** Pattern in 4/4:
- Beat 1: Root note (C2)
- Beat 2: 5th or 10th (G2 or E3)
- Beat 3: Chord (E3-G3-C4)
- Beat 4: Same chord or variation

**Practical Tip:** Creates bouncy, energetic feel. Left hand "strides" across keyboard. Common in boogie-woogie and early jazz.

### 70. Octave Playing
**Concept:** Playing same note in multiple octaves for power and thickness.
**Piano Roll Application:** Right hand melody doubled in octaves:
- Single: C5-D5-E5
- Octaves: C4+C5, D4+D5, E4+E5

**Practical Tip:** Requires hand span - typically pinky + thumb. Adds drama and volume. Common in classical climaxes and gospel.

### 71. Broken Chords and Arpeggios
**Concept:** Playing chord notes sequentially rather than simultaneously.
**Piano Roll Application:** C major broken chord patterns:
- Ascending: C3-E3-G3-C4
- Alberti bass: C3-G3-E3-G3 (classical accompaniment)
- Rolling: C3-E3-G3-E3-C3-E3-G3-E3

**Practical Tip:** Creates movement without changing harmony. Essential for piano accompaniment patterns.

### 72. Two-Hand Independence
**Concept:** Each hand plays different rhythm, melody, or pattern simultaneously.
**Piano Roll Application:** Layer two patterns:
- Left hand: Steady quarter note bass line
- Right hand: Syncopated melody or off-beat chords

**Practical Tip:** Program each hand separately. Test that patterns are physically playable. Advanced pianists can handle complex polyrhythms.

### 73. Piano Pedaling (Sustain)
**Concept:** Sustain pedal extends note duration, blends harmonies, adds resonance.
**Piano Roll Application:** Use MIDI CC64 (sustain pedal) or extend note lengths:
- No pedal: Notes release immediately (staccato)
- With pedal: Notes ring together until pedal release

**Practical Tip:** Change pedal with each chord change to avoid muddiness. Half-pedaling = subtle sustain (MIDI value 40-60).

### 74. Block Chords vs. Rolled Chords
**Concept:** Block = all notes simultaneously. Rolled = notes played rapidly in succession (arpeggiated).
**Piano Roll Application:**
- Block chord: C3, E3, G3 all start at same grid position
- Rolled chord: C3, E3 (+5ms), G3 (+10ms) for upward roll

**Practical Tip:** Roll chords for romantic, flowing feel. Block chords for rhythmic, percussive sound. Jazz often uses rolled voicings.

### 75. Crossover and Crossunder Technique
**Concept:** One hand crosses over/under the other to access different register or play scale runs.
**Piano Roll Application:** Usually right hand crosses over left for low melodic notes, or left crosses over for high accompaniment.
**Practical Tip:** When programming, ensure notes don't overlap in impossible ways. Crossovers work best for single-note lines, not chords.

### 76. Grace Notes and Ornaments
**Concept:** Quick decorative notes: grace notes, trills, mordents, turns.
**Piano Roll Application:**
- Grace note: Very short note (1/32 or shorter) just before main note, low velocity
- Trill: Rapid alternation between two adjacent notes
- Mordent: Main note, upper/lower neighbor, back to main

**Practical Tip:** Grace notes add classical/jazz flavor. Keep them quiet (velocity 50-70) and very short.

### 77. Glissando
**Concept:** Sliding hand rapidly across keys (usually white keys only).
**Piano Roll Application:** Create chromatic or diatonic run:
- White key gliss in C major: C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C (ascending/descending)
- Black key gliss: pentatonic scale (C#-D#-F#-G#-A#)

**Practical Tip:** Start each note slightly offset (2-5ms apart). Increase velocity toward end for realistic acceleration.

### 78. Tremolo
**Concept:** Rapid repetition of single note or alternating between two notes.
**Piano Roll Application:**
- Single note tremolo: C5 repeated as fast as 1/32 or 1/64 notes
- Interval tremolo: C5-G5 alternating rapidly

**Practical Tip:** Creates tension and drama. Slight velocity variation prevents robotic sound. Common in romantic/cinematic piano.

### 79. Parallel Chords (Planing)
**Concept:** Moving a chord shape up/down in parallel motion (all voices move by same interval).
**Piano Roll Application:** Take C-E-G shape, move entire shape up chromatically:
- Bar 1: C4-E4-G4
- Bar 2: C#4-F4-G#4
- Bar 3: D4-F#4-A4

**Practical Tip:** Creates impressionistic, Debussy-like sound. Breaks traditional voice leading rules but sounds lush.

### 80. Contrary Motion
**Concept:** Hands move in opposite directions (one up, one down).
**Piano Roll Application:**
- Left hand descends: C3-B2-A2-G2
- Right hand ascends: E4-F4-G4-A4

**Practical Tip:** Creates balance and sophistication. Classical composers use extensively. Sounds complex but easier to play than it looks.

### 81. Chopin Waltz Accompaniment
**Concept:** Left hand pattern for 3/4 waltz time: bass note on beat 1, chord on beats 2-3.
**Piano Roll Application:** In 3/4 time, C major:
- Beat 1: C2 (root bass note)
- Beats 2-3: E3-G3-C4 (chord)

**Practical Tip:** Classic romantic piano sound. Bass note emphasizes downbeat, chords create lilting dance feel.

### 82. Gospel Piano Runs
**Concept:** Rapid scalar or chromatic runs connecting chords, heavily used in gospel/R&B.
**Piano Roll Application:** Ascending run from C4 to C5 using major scale + chromatic passing tones:
- C4-D4-Eb4-E4-F4-G4-Ab4-A4-Bb4-B4-C5 (mix of scale and chromatic)

**Practical Tip:** Runs typically lead into downbeat. Increase velocity toward end. Often ends on chord tone.

### 83. Comping (Jazz Piano)
**Concept:** Rhythmic, syncopated chord voicings that "comp" (accompany) soloist.
**Piano Roll Application:** Play 7th chord shells (3rd and 7th) on off-beats:
- Beat 2+: G3-B3 (for Cmaj7, playing 5th and 7th)
- Beat 4+: E3-B3 (3rd and 7th)

**Practical Tip:** Leave space - don't play on every beat. Syncopate and accent off-beats. Omit roots if bass is present.

### 84. Montuno Pattern (Latin Piano)
**Concept:** Syncopated, repetitive ostinato used in salsa, Latin jazz.
**Piano Roll Application:** 2-bar pattern in C:
- C4-E4-G4 (on 1, on 2-and, on 4)
- Repeat with slight rhythmic variations

**Practical Tip:** Right hand plays pattern, left hand plays bass tumbao. Creates infectious groove. Stay in middle register (C4-C5).

### 85. Two-Note Voicings (Shell Voicings)
**Concept:** Playing only essential chord tones (typically 3rd and 7th) instead of full chord.
**Piano Roll Application:** Cmaj7 shell voicings:
- Root position: E3-B3 (3rd and 7th)
- Inversion: B3-E4

**Practical Tip:** Less is more in jazz. Shell voicings leave room for bass and other instruments. Add color tones (9th, 13th) occasionally.

### 86. Sustain, Sostenuto, and Una Corda Pedals
**Concept:** Piano has 3 pedals: sustain (right), sostenuto (middle), una corda/soft (left).
**Piano Roll Application:**
- CC64: Sustain (most important)
- CC66: Sostenuto (sustains only notes already held)
- CC67: Soft pedal (reduces volume and changes timbre)

**Practical Tip:** Most MIDI controllers only send CC64. Sostenuto allows bass note to ring while playing staccato melody above.

### 87. Velocity Layering and Dynamics
**Concept:** Piano is dynamic instrument - from pppp (very quiet) to ffff (very loud). Velocity affects timbre, not just volume.
**Piano Roll Application:** Dynamic range:
- pp (40-60): Soft, warm tone
- mf (70-90): Medium, conversational
- ff (100-127): Bright, powerful, more overtones

**Practical Tip:** Vary velocity for musical expression. Classical piano requires huge dynamic range. Avoid constant velocity.

## Practice Exercises

### Beginner Exercises
1. **Scale Practice:** Program each major and minor scale, one per key
2. **Chord Building:** Build all diatonic triads in a major key
3. **Interval Training:** Create each interval type from a root note
4. **Simple Melody:** Write 8-bar melody using only pentatonic scale

### Intermediate Exercises
5. **Chord Progressions:** Program ii-V-I in all 12 keys
6. **Voice Leading:** Create smooth transitions between I-IV-V-I using inversions
7. **Harmonization:** Take a melody and harmonize in 3rds
8. **Rhythm Variation:** Create same melody with different rhythmic patterns

### Advanced Exercises
9. **Modal Composition:** Write chord progression in Dorian mode
10. **Reharmonization:** Take simple progression (I-vi-IV-V) and substitute with extended chords
11. **Countermelody:** Write independent countermelody to existing melody
12. **Polyrhythmic Pattern:** Create pattern with 3:4 polyrhythm

## Piano Roll Tips and Workflows

### Workflow Tips
- **Use MIDI Reference Scales:** Most DAWs can highlight scale notes - reduces wrong notes
- **Humanize:** Slightly randomize timing and velocity for natural feel
- **Layer Octaves:** Double melodies at different octaves for fuller sound
- **Chord Voicing:** Don't play all notes in same octave - spread across 2-3 octaves
- **Ghost Notes:** Add very quiet notes (low velocity) for groove (especially in hi-hats/percussion)

### Common Shortcuts (DAW-Dependent)
- Alt/Option + Drag: Duplicate notes/patterns
- Ctrl/Cmd + A: Select all notes
- Ctrl/Cmd + D: Duplicate selection
- Hold Ctrl/Cmd while dragging: Fine adjustment (ignore snap)
- Shift + Click: Select multiple notes

### Analysis Exercise
Take your favorite song and:
1. Identify the key and scale
2. Chart out the chord progression using Roman numerals
3. Analyze the melody - where are the leaps? Where is stepwise motion?
4. Note any borrowed chords or modal interchange
5. Examine the rhythm - is there syncopation? What's the groove?

## Conclusion

Mastering these 50 concepts will give you a comprehensive foundation for creating music in a piano roll environment. Remember:

- **Theory is a tool, not a rule** - knowing when to break conventions is as important as knowing them
- **Train your ear** - the visual nature of piano roll is helpful, but developing your ear is crucial
- **Analyze music you love** - recreate sections in piano roll to understand how they work
- **Experiment constantly** - the piano roll makes experimentation easy - use it!
- **Context matters** - what works in jazz might not work in EDM, and vice versa

Keep this guide handy as a reference while producing. The more you apply these concepts, the more intuitive they'll become.

Happy music making! 🎹🎵
